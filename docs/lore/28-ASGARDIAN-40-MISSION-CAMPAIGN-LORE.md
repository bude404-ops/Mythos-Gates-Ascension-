# Asgardian Full Faction Campaign Lore — v0.5.0

The Asgardian campaign begins when the Thunder-Oath Gate reopens across the storm-bridge remnants of the old world. The Asgardian do not treat the Gate as a puzzle to be solved. They treat it as an oath-site — a place where a promise was broken, and broken promises have weight.

The campaign begins on the broken rune arrival span where the Bifrost once connected the Nine Realms. After the Ascension Collapse, the bridge shattered. The Gate's reopening does not repair the bridge — it reveals the fracture. The chosen deity must cross a span held together by oath-memory: every stone is a promise, every gap is a broken vow, and every step forward requires the walker to prove they still hold.

Across five chapters, the campaign moves from oath reactivation through banner conflict, world-root decay, and a final confrontation where the Gate itself has become an oath that no one alive remembers making.

Elite missions reinterpret these events as sharper oath trials. The Gate has learned from the Normal campaign. It uses the deity's own promises as weapons, changes which oaths are still binding mid-mission, and forces the deity to win without swearing any new vows.

The finale leaves the Gate standing but not sealed. The Asgardian win the right to hold the oath, not the right to break it.

## Campaign Spine

### Chapter 1 — Oath Under Thunder
The Gate reopens across the broken Bifrost span. The first thing the deity feels is the weight of old oaths pressing against the storm-iron bridge. Every stone in the span remembers a promise made before the Collapse — and every promise is now calling in its debt. Hrafn Storm-Witness arrives to read the oaths, not to enforce them. He explains: the bridge does not test strength. It tests whether the walker still holds what they swore to hold. The Hollow oathless draugr emerge from the bridge fractures — undead who broke their oaths in life and now serve the Gap. The deity must cross while the bridge tests their resolve.

**Mission Focus:** Oath reactivation. Establish that the Asgardian Gate is an oath-site, not a portal. First Hollow contact through oathless draugr. The weight of promises as terrain pressure.

### Chapter 2 — The Banner That Forgot Its King
Deep in the storm-bridge route, a Forgotten banner rises from pre-Collapse stone. It carries the memory of an Asgardian king who swore to hold the bridge — and failed. The banner does not attack; it accuses. It shows the deity the moment the oath was broken and asks: will you hold where they did not? The Hollow oath-eaters use the banner's accusation to create doubt — and in the Asgardian Gate, doubt is terrain. The bridge begins to dissolve under the deity's feet as the oath-eaters feed on the uncertainty. The deity must fight while standing on a surface that only exists because they believe it does.

**Mission Focus:** Forgotten banner. The bridge dissolves under doubt. Oath-eaters feed on uncertainty. The deity must fight while maintaining absolute conviction — or fall.

### Chapter 3 — Marble Law, Iron Oath
The storm-bridge route intersects with the Olympian trial road. The Forgotten banner's testimony has drawn the Olympian legal system into Asgardian oath-space. Olympian trial law says: excellence must be witnessed. Asgardian oath law says: a promise held in darkness is still a promise. These two systems do not agree on what makes power legitimate. The deity must navigate a battlefield where the rules change depending on which legal tradition holds jurisdiction over the current position. Hollow victory parasites feed on the jurisdictional chaos, imitating both legal systems to drain meaning from both.

**Mission Focus:** Cross-faction collision. Olympian vs Asgardian legal systems. Jurisdiction changes mid-mission based on position. The deity must master two rule sets or break both.

### Chapter 4 — The Bridge Remembers Blood
The storm-bridge opens into the blood-memory chamber — a place where the bridge records every battle fought on its surface. The deity's own past battles are recorded here, and the Hollow has learned to read them. The Hollow rune-bound wolves emerge from the blood-memory, wearing the shapes of every enemy the deity has ever defeated. They do not have the enemies' powers — they have the deity's own tactics reflected back. The deity must fight their own combat history while the bridge replays every wound they have ever taken.

**Mission Focus:** Blood-memory. The deity fights echoes of their own battles. Hollow rune-bound wolves use the deity's tactics against them. The bridge replays past wounds as terrain hazards.

### Chapter 5 — The Thunder Tribunal Usurper
The Gate's final chamber is the Thunder Tribunal — the court where broken oaths are judged. The Thunder Tribunal Usurper — a Hollow entity wearing the armor of the first Asgardian oath-keeper — presides. It has consumed enough oath-law to judge the deity by every promise they have ever made, including promises they did not know they were making. The Usurper's power is simple: it can declare any oath void. It begins voiding the deity's oath to their faction, to their people, to themselves. The deity must win not by making new oaths — which the Usurper can void — but by acting without oaths. By holding the bridge through action alone, not through words.

**Mission Focus:** Final confrontation. Boss: The Thunder Tribunal Usurper. Victory condition: the deity must win by acting without swearing — proving that a promise kept in silence is still a promise kept. The bridge holds because the deity holds it, not because they said they would.

## Elite Campaign

Elite missions reinterpret the campaign as a sharper oath trial. The Gate has learned from the Normal run:
- Oath pressure now targets the deity's Normal campaign promises specifically
- The Forgotten banner shows the deity's Normal campaign failures, not ancient ones
- Jurisdiction changes faster in the cross-faction zones
- The Thunder Tribunal Usurper can void the deity's Elite build and loadout, forcing victory with base abilities

The finale proves that holding was not accidental. The deity must hold the bridge twice — once against the oath, once against the oath's memory of being held.
