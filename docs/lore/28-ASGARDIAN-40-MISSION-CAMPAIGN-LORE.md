# Asgardian Full Faction Campaign Lore — v0.5.0


## Avatar System

Players do not control the deities directly. Each player channels an **Avatar** — a divine projection of their chosen deity. The avatar carries the deity's power, faith triggers, and faction bonuses into combat, but the deity itself remains protected in its domain.

- **Progression**: Avatars grow stronger through **Belief** (faction-core faith) and **Influence** (deity-specific faith path), earned by completing missions and conquering realms.
- **Death**: If an avatar falls, it respawns at the deity's domain. The deity is unharmed. Progression is preserved with a 10-20% Belief/Influence penalty.
- **Scale**: Avatars maintain god-scale proportions in all realms — they tower over buildings, landscapes, and mortal structures.
- **Solo Combat**: Each avatar fights alone. No allies, no healing companions. Faith triggers are self-only.
- **Realm Advantage**: Avatars fight with full power in their deity's home realm. Earth is neutral ground — no faction holds advantage. Faction realms are reserved for special events.


The Asgardian campaign begins when the Thunder-Oath Gate reopens across the storm-bridge remnants of the old world. The Asgardian do not treat the Gate as a puzzle to be solved. They treat it as an oath-site — a place where a promise was broken, and broken promises have weight.

The campaign begins on the broken rune arrival span where the Bifrost once connected the Nine Realms. After the Ascension Collapse, the bridge shattered. The Gate's reopening does not repair the bridge — it reveals the fracture. The avatar must cross a span held together by oath-memory: every stone is a promise, every gap is a broken vow, and every step forward requires the Avatar to prove they still hold. Every oath fulfilled strengthens the belief connection between player and Deity.

Across five chapters, the campaign moves from oath reactivation through banner conflict, world-root decay, and a final confrontation where the Forgotten Standard Bearer — a Hollow entity wearing the armor of the first oath-keeper — stands at the end of the bridge, voiding every promise the deity has ever made.

Elite missions reinterpret these events as sharper oath trials. The Gate has learned from the Normal campaign. It uses the deity's own promises as weapons, changes which oaths are still binding mid-mission, and forces the avatar to win without swearing any new vows.

The finale leaves the Gate standing but not sealed. The Asgardian win the right to hold the oath, not the right to break it.


## MYTHOLOGICAL EARTH CAMPAIGN

**Core Direction**: All campaign missions take place on Earth — specifically in Ancient Scandinavia during the circa 500 CE — Migration Age (pre-Viking). Each faction fights in the geographic region where their mythology originates, set in an ancient historical timeline. The enemy is the **Hollow** — void creatures invading through the Gate.

### Why Ancient Scandinavia?

The deities drew their original power from the cultures that worshipped them. When the Gate opened on Earth, it fractured the very lands where these mythologies were born. The Hollow exploit these ancient Gate-wounds. Each deity sends their avatar to defend their mythological homeland — the place where human belief first gave them form.

### Earth Environments by Chapter:

| Chapter | Location | Visual Description |
|---------|----------|-------------------|
| Ch1 | Frozen Fjord | ice-choked waters between black cliffs, longhouse settlements with turf roofs cracking under Gate-pressure, rune stones bleeding white energy |
| Ch2 | Burial Mounds | ship graves half-consumed by void-mist, runic standing stones rearranged by Gate energy, frozen barrows cracking open to reveal Hollow-corrupted remains |
| Ch3 | Thingvellir | ancient assembly grounds between rift valley walls, the law rock split by a Gate fracture, the canyon bleeding white energy through its basalt cracks |
| Ch4 | The Great Forest | ancient pine forest dark as night, sacrificial groves where trees have turned to void-crystal, the deep woods where Hollow creatures hunt |
| Ch5 | Yggdrasil Root | the world tree's exposed root system in the frozen north — massive roots cracking through bedrock, void-mist pouring from between the bark, the Gate nexus pulsing where root meets stone |

### The Enemy: The Hollow

All campaign enemies are **Hollow** — void creatures that emerge through the Gate. They are not the faction deities or their armies. The Hollow are the antithesis of all divine creation — formless void-mist entities anchored by Gate stone fragments. They consume belief, dissolve divine energy, and seek to unmake the Gate itself.

### Earth is Neutral Ground

No faction advantage or penalty applies. The avatar fights with full power on Earth — but the Hollow are strongest at the Gate's breach points. The campaign difficulty scales with Hollow density, not faction opposition.

### Faction Realms (Events Only)

The deity's actual realm (Ancient Scandinavia) is accessible only through special events — realm raids, faction wars, seasonal content, and world boss encounters. The campaign stays on Earth.

## Campaign Spine

### Chapter 1 — Oath Under Thunder
The Gate reopens across the broken Bifrost span. The first thing the deity feels is the weight of old oaths pressing against the storm-iron bridge. Every stone in the span remembers a promise made before the Collapse — and every promise is now calling in its debt. Hrafn Storm-Witness arrives to read the oaths, not to enforce them. He explains: the bridge does not test strength. It tests whether the walker still holds what they swore to hold. The Hollow oathless draugr emerge from the bridge fractures — undead who broke their oaths in life and now serve the Gap. The deity must cross while the bridge tests their resolve.

**Mission Focus:** Oath reactivation. Establish that the Asgardian Gate is an oath-site, not a portal. First Hollow contact through oathless draugr. The weight of promises as terrain pressure.

### Chapter 2 — The Banner That Forgot Its King
Deep in the storm-bridge route, a Forgotten banner rises from pre-Collapse stone. It carries the memory of an Asgardian king who swore to hold the bridge — and failed. The banner does not attack; it accuses. It shows the deity the moment the oath was broken and asks: will you hold where they did not? The Hollow oath-eaters use the banner's accusation to create doubt — and in the Asgardian Gate, doubt is terrain. The bridge begins to dissolve under the deity's feet as the oath-eaters feed on the uncertainty. The deity must fight while standing on a surface that only exists because they believe it does.

**Mission Focus:** Forgotten banner. The bridge dissolves under doubt. Oath-eaters feed on uncertainty. The deity must fight while maintaining absolute conviction — or fall.

### Chapter 3 — Marble Law, Iron Oath
The storm-bridge route intersects with the Olympian trial road. The Forgotten banner's testimony has drawn the Olympian legal system into Asgardian oath-space. Olympian trial law says: excellence must be witnessed. Asgardian oath law says: a promise held in darkness is still a promise. These two systems do not agree on what makes power legitimate. The deity must navigate a battlefield where the rules change depending on which legal tradition holds jurisdiction over the current position. Hollow victory parasites feed on the jurisdictional chaos, imitating both legal systems to drain meaning from both.

**Mission Focus:** Cross-faction collision. Olympian vs Asgardian legal systems. Jurisdiction changes mid-mission based on position. The deity must master two rule sets or break both.

### Chapter 4 — The Bridge Remembers Blood
The storm-bridge opens into the blood-memory chamber — a place where the bridge records every battle fought on its surface. The deity's own past battles are recorded here, and the Hollow has learned to read them. The Hollow rune-bound wolves emerge from the blood-memory, wearing the shapes of every enemy the deity has ever defeated. They do not have the enemies' powers — they have the deity's own tactics reflected back. The deity must fight their own combat history while the bridge replays every wound they have ever taken.

**Mission Focus:** Blood-memory. The deity fights echoes of their own battles. Hollow rune-bound wolves use the deity's tactics against them. The bridge replays past wounds as terrain hazards.

### Chapter 5 — The Forgotten Standard Bearer
The Gate's final chamber is the Standard Bearer Storm Keep — a storm-wrapped fortress at the end of the broken bridge where the first Asgardian oath-keeper once held the line. The Forgotten Standard Bearer waits there: a Hollow entity wearing the armor of that first oath-keeper, still holding the banner they died defending. It has consumed enough oath-law to declare any promise void. It begins voiding the deity's oath to their faction, to their people, to themselves. The deity cannot fight it with new oaths — the Standard Bearer can void any vow spoken in its presence. The deity must win not by making new oaths but by acting without words. By holding the bridge through action alone, by standing where the Standard Bearer once fell.

**Mission Focus:** Final confrontation. Boss: The Forgotten Standard Bearer. Victory condition: the deity must win by acting without swearing — proving that a promise kept in silence is still a promise kept. The bridge holds because the deity holds it, not because they said they would.

## Elite Campaign

Elite missions reinterpret the campaign as a sharper oath trial. The Gate has learned from the Normal run:
- Oath pressure now targets the deity's Normal campaign promises specifically
- The Forgotten banner shows the deity's Normal campaign failures, not ancient ones
- Jurisdiction changes faster in the cross-faction zones
- The Forgotten Standard Bearer can void the deity's Elite build and loadout, forcing victory with base abilities

The finale proves that holding was not accidental. The deity must hold the bridge twice — once against the oath, once against the oath's memory of being held.
