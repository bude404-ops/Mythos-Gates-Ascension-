# Infernal Dominion Full Faction Campaign Lore — v0.5.0


## Avatar System

Players do not control the deities directly. Each player channels an **Avatar** — a divine projection of their chosen deity. The avatar carries the deity's power, faith triggers, and faction bonuses into combat, but the deity itself remains protected in its domain.

- **Progression**: Avatars grow stronger through **Belief** (faction-core faith) and **Influence** (deity-specific faith path), earned by completing missions and conquering realms.
- **Death**: If an avatar falls, it respawns at the deity's domain. The deity is unharmed. Progression is preserved with a 10-20% Belief/Influence penalty.
- **Scale**: Avatars maintain god-scale proportions in all realms — they tower over buildings, landscapes, and mortal structures.
- **Solo Combat**: Each avatar fights alone. No allies, no healing companions. Faith triggers are self-only.
- **Earth Campaigns**: All missions take place in the deity's mythological homeland on ancient Earth. Earth is neutral ground — no faction advantage or penalty. Faction realms are reserved for special events only.


The Infernal Dominion campaign begins when the Black-Iron Gate reopens beneath a court of execution sigils. The Dominion does not treat the Gate as a threat to be defeated. It treats the Gate as an opportunity to be negotiated — and negotiation is the Dominion's native language.

The campaign begins in the contract courts below the Black-Iron Gate where every tactical victory creates a record, every record creates leverage, and every leverage point can become a chain. Varak Chain-Magistrate offers information to the Avatar, passage, and power — never freely, never falsely. The danger is not that he lies. The danger is that he tells the truth in a shape that benefits Dominion law.

Across five chapters, the campaign moves from contract negotiation through debt collection, Gateborn flame distortion, Hollow clause-eaters, and a final confrontation where the Gate has become the ultimate creditor — and it has come to collect.

Elite missions reinterpret these events as sharper contract trials. The Gate has learned from the Normal campaign. It uses the deity's own victories as leverage, rewrites contract terms mid-mission, and forces the avatar to win without creating any new debts.

The finale leaves the Gate bound but not broken. The Dominion win the right to renegotiate, not the right to own.


## MYTHOLOGICAL EARTH CAMPAIGN

**Core Direction**: All campaign missions take place on Earth — specifically in Ancient Mesopotamia — The Underworld during the circa 3000 BCE — Sumerian underworld (Kur/Irkalla). Each faction fights in the geographic region where their mythology originates, set in an ancient historical timeline. The enemy is the **Hollow** — void creatures invading through the Gate.

### Why Ancient Mesopotamia — The Underworld?

The deities drew their original power from the cultures that worshipped them. When the Gate opened on Earth, it fractured the very lands where these mythologies were born. The Hollow exploit these ancient Gate-wounds. Each deity sends their avatar to defend their mythological homeland — the place where human belief first gave them form.

### Earth Environments by Chapter:

| Chapter | Location | Visual Description |
|---------|----------|-------------------|
| Ch1 | Cursed Ruins | burned city remains from the ancient world, cracked mudbrick walls weeping void-mist, sulfur vents breaking through flagstones, skeletal architecture under blood-red sky |
| Ch2 | The Underworld Gates | descending stone passages into Kur, obsidian doors cracked by Gate pressure, flame-lit tunnels now flickering with anti-light, the river Hubur running black with void-taint |
| Ch3 | Throne of Ash | destroyed palace of the ancient dead, broken statues of forgotten kings, ash-filled halls where Hollow presence thickens, the throne room split open by a Gate eruption |
| Ch4 | The Lake of Fire | volcanic crater in the underworld depths, molten rock mixing with void-mist, obsidian spires rising from the flames |
| Ch5 | The Abyss | the deepest point of the ancient underworld, total darkness pierced only by Gate-fractures, the Gate nexus pulsing in the absolute dark — the antithesis of creation |

### The Enemy: The Hollow

All campaign enemies are **Hollow** — void creatures that emerge through the Gate. They are not the faction deities or their armies. The Hollow are the antithesis of all divine creation — formless void-mist entities anchored by Gate stone fragments. They consume belief, dissolve divine energy, and seek to unmake the Gate itself.

### Earth is Neutral Ground

No faction advantage or penalty applies. The avatar fights with full power on Earth — but the Hollow are strongest at the Gate's breach points. The campaign difficulty scales with Hollow density, not faction opposition.

### Faction Realms (Events Only)

The deity's actual realm (Ancient Mesopotamia — Underworld) is accessible only through special events — realm raids, faction wars, seasonal content, and world boss encounters. The campaign stays on Earth.

## Campaign Spine

### Chapter 1 — Black-Iron Threshold
The Gate reopens beneath the execution sigil court. The first thing the deity sees is a contract floating in the air above the Gate: terms of entry, written in fire. The contract is not hostile; it is explicit. It lists what the Gate will take in exchange for passage. Varak Chain-Magistrate arrives to witness the signing — not to enforce, but to ensure both parties understand the terms. The Hollow clause-eaters emerge from the Gate's margins, consuming the contract text itself to create loopholes that neither party agreed to. The deity must fight the clause-eaters while protecting the contract — because a contract without terms is a debt without limit.

**Mission Focus:** Contract negotiation. Establish that the Dominion operates through explicit law, not deception. First Hollow contact through contract corruption.

### Chapter 2 — The Debt Ledger
Deep in the Black-Iron Gate route, the deity discovers the Debt Ledger — a record of every transaction the Gate has ever processed, including debts from before the Ascension Collapse. The ledger is not balanced. The Collapse erased entire civilizations without clearing their debts, and now the Gate is trying to collect from their successors. The deity's own faction carries inherited debt from the pre-Collapse era. The False Pact Wardens — Hollow entities wearing Infernal judge robes — use the ledger to claim the deity owes more than they can pay. The deity must fight through the debt collection process while the ledger keeps adding new charges for every action they take.

**Mission Focus:** Inherited debt. The deity fights while the battlefield records every action as a new charge. The question: can you win a battle that costs more than it earns?

### Chapter 3 — Ash Market
The route opens into the Ash Market — a pre-Collapse trading post where the Infernal Dominion once exchanged power with other Realms. The market is now hollowed out but still functional. The Chain-Bound Colossus — a Hollow entity made from fused contract chains — patrols the market, enforcing deals that no longer have two parties. The deity must navigate a space where every treasure chest has a price, every shrine node has a fee, and every step forward adds to the debt. But the Ash Market also offers something no other route does: the ability to trade debt. The deity can shift their debt onto Hollow entities by defeating them in the market's terms.

**Mission Focus:** Ash Market. Everything has a price. The deity can trade debt onto enemies. The Chain-Bound Colossus as elite encounter. Risk: every trade creates a new relationship — and relationships in the Dominion are chains.

### Chapter 4 — The Execution Sigil Crisis
The Gate's contract clauses have been rewritten by the Hollow clause-eaters. Every execution sigil in the route now targets the deity instead of the enemies. The Ash Court Usurper — a Hollow entity that has consumed enough Dominion law to issue execution orders — uses the rewritten sigils to drain the deity's power directly. The deity must fight in a battlefield where their own faction's law has been turned against them. The only counter: the deity must rewrite the execution sigils mid-combat, changing who they target — a process that requires holding still while enemies attack.

**Mission Focus:** Law inversion. The deity's own faction law is weaponized against them. The Ash Court Usurper. The deity must rewrite contracts under fire.

### Chapter 5 — The Volcanic Glass Magistrate
The Gate's final chamber is a courtroom made of volcanic glass. The Volcanic Glass Magistrate — a Hollow entity wearing the robes of the first Infernal judge — presides. It has no interest in truth; it has interest in collection. It presents the deity's total debt: every action taken during the campaign, every contract signed, every deal made in the Ash Market. The debt is calculated to be exactly more than the avatar can pay. The only way to win is not to pay — but to prove that the Magistrate's own authority is itself a debt it cannot justify. The deity must turn the court's law against the court itself.

**Mission Focus:** Final confrontation. Boss: The Volcanic Glass Magistrate. Victory condition: the deity must win by proving the court has no authority to collect — because the court itself was created by a contract that the Hollow has already eaten. The debt is void because the creditor no longer legally exists.

## Elite Campaign

Elite missions reinterpret the campaign as a sharper contract trial. The Gate has learned from the Normal run:
- The initial contract now includes the deity's Normal campaign performance as a baseline
- The Debt Ledger starts pre-charged with the deity's Normal campaign actions
- The Ash Market prices are higher and the trade options are narrower
- The Volcanic Glass Magistrate presents the Elite debt as cumulative — Normal + Elite — and the deity must void both

The finale proves that the Dominion's law is not just for the weak. The deity must win the court twice — once against the Gate's charges, once against the Gate's memory of being outmaneuvered.
