# Tuatha Full Faction Campaign Lore — v0.5.0


## Avatar System

Players do not control the deities directly. Each player channels an **Avatar** — a divine projection of their chosen deity. The avatar carries the deity's power, faith triggers, and faction bonuses into combat, but the deity itself remains protected in its domain.

- **Progression**: Avatars grow stronger through **Belief** (faction-core faith) and **Influence** (deity-specific faith path), earned by completing missions and conquering realms.
- **Death**: If an avatar falls, it respawns at the deity's domain. The deity is unharmed. Progression is preserved with a 10-20% Belief/Influence penalty.
- **Scale**: Avatars maintain god-scale proportions in all realms — they tower over buildings, landscapes, and mortal structures.
- **Solo Combat**: Each avatar fights alone. No allies, no healing companions. Faith triggers are self-only.
- **Earth Campaigns**: All missions take place in the deity's mythological homeland on ancient Earth. Earth is neutral ground — no faction advantage or penalty. Faction realms are reserved for special events only.


The Tuatha campaign begins when the Silver-Root Gate reopens beneath the living terrain of Avalora. The Tuatha do not treat the Gate as a machine or a legal contract. They treat it as a wound in a living thing — and wounds in living things either heal or fester.

The campaign begins in the root network below a damaged Gate where living wood has grown around impossible pressure. The roots did not invade the ruins; they held them together. Every branch, stone, and moonlit pool stores part of what the Collapse tried to erase. Maeve Root-Memory guides the Avatar through terrain that remembers every army it has eaten and every world it has saved by refusing to let dead roads die.

Across five chapters, the campaign moves from root restoration through Hollow rot, Beast Realm predation, and a final confrontation where the Gate has grown into the living terrain so deeply that removing it would kill the forest.

Elite missions reinterpret these events as sharper adaptive trials. The wound has learned from the Normal campaign. It changes which roots are safe, corrupts the terrain memory, and forces the avatar to adapt faster than the forest can heal.

The finale leaves the Gate integrated but not removed. The Tuatha win the right to let the scar become part of the forest, not the right to pretend it never happened.


## MYTHOLOGICAL EARTH CAMPAIGN

**Core Direction**: All campaign missions take place on Earth — specifically in Ancient Ireland during the circa 1000 BCE — Celtic Bronze Age. Each faction fights in the geographic region where their mythology originates, set in an ancient historical timeline. The enemy is the **Hollow** — void creatures invading through the Gate.

### Why Ancient Ireland?

The deities drew their original power from the cultures that worshipped them. When the Gate opened on Earth, it fractured the very lands where these mythologies were born. The Hollow exploit these ancient Gate-wounds. Each deity sends their avatar to defend their mythological homeland — the place where human belief first gave them form.

### Earth Environments by Chapter:

| Chapter | Location | Visual Description |
|---------|----------|-------------------|
| Ch1 | Misted Coastline | green cliffs above the wild Atlantic, Celtic ringforts with wattle walls cracking under Gate-pressure, standing stones bleeding white energy |
| Ch2 | Sacred Grove | ancient oaks older than memory, druidic stone circles disrupted by void-fissures, ogham-covered stones floating in anti-gravity, mist thick with Hollow presence |
| Ch3 | Hill Fort | earthen ramparts and thatched roundhouses, ceremonial fire pits now burning with anti-light, ogham stones rearranged by Gate energy |
| Ch4 | Passage Tombs | megalithic passage tombs like Newgrange, spiral carvings on stone slabs bleeding void-mist, underground chambers extending into impossible depths |
| Ch5 | Giant's Causeway | basalt columns rising from the sea, hexagonal stones cracking with void-energy, the coast where myth meets reality, the Gate nexus pulsing beneath the waves |

### The Enemy: The Hollow

All campaign enemies are **Hollow** — void creatures that emerge through the Gate. They are not the faction deities or their armies. The Hollow are the antithesis of all divine creation — formless void-mist entities anchored by Gate stone fragments. They consume belief, dissolve divine energy, and seek to unmake the Gate itself.

### Earth is Neutral Ground

No faction advantage or penalty applies. The avatar fights with full power on Earth — but the Hollow are strongest at the Gate's breach points. The campaign difficulty scales with Hollow density, not faction opposition.

### Faction Realms (Events Only)

The deity's actual realm (Ancient Ireland) is accessible only through special events — realm raids, faction wars, seasonal content, and world boss encounters. The campaign stays on Earth.

## Campaign Spine

### Chapter 1 — Silver-Root Threshold
The Gate reopens beneath the oldest grove in Avalora. The roots around it have been holding the ruins together for millennia, but the Gate's reopening sends new pressure through the root network — pressure that the living terrain cannot absorb without changing shape. Maeve Root-Memory arrives to read the terrain's response. She does not call the land innocent; it has eaten armies. But it has also saved worlds. The avatar must enter the root network and stabilize the first threshold before the Hollow rot spreads through the root memory into the rest of Avalora.

**Mission Focus:** Root restoration. Establish that nature in Mythos Gates is not scenery — it is an extradimensional intelligence with old injuries. First Hollow contact through root-rot.

### Chapter 2 — The Geas Labyrinth
The root network opens into a geas labyrinth — a living maze grown from ancient promises, broken oaths, and bloodline debts that the forest has absorbed over millennia. The labyrinth does not trap; it tests whether the walker understands which promises still have roots and which have rotted. Hollow changelings use the labyrinth to swap the deity's terrain memory, making safe roots appear dangerous and rotten roots appear stable. The deity must navigate by reading the terrain, not by trusting the path.

**Mission Focus:** Geas labyrinth. Living maze of promises. Hollow changelings corrupt terrain memory. The deity must adapt to a battlefield that keeps changing what it remembers.

### Chapter 3 — Beast Realm Predation
Deep in the root network, Beast Realm predators have nested in the wounded roots. They are not Hollow; they are ancient creatures drawn to the Gate's energy. The Beast Realm Maneater, the Elder Root Ogre, and the Bog Sidhe Hexer have claimed territory that the Tuatha need to restore. The avatar must fight through the nesting grounds without destroying the roots the predators are using as shelter — because the roots are also holding the Gate stable. The predator echo whispers: "Warm law. Soft memory. Breakable paths."

**Mission Focus:** Beast Realm incursion. The deity must fight creatures while protecting the terrain they are damaging. The question: does the forest have the right to reject creatures that are part of its ecosystem?

### Chapter 4 — The Scar That Remembers
The root network opens into the oldest part of Avalora — a scar from the original Ascension Collapse that the forest has never healed. The scar remembers everything it lost: civilizations, names, roads, gods. The Hollow uses the scar's memory to create perfect copies of lost Tuatha deities and sends them against the chosen one. These are not enemies; they are ghosts of who the forest lost. The deity must fight the memory of their own people while the terrain weeps.

**Mission Focus:** Scar memory. Ghost copies of lost deities. The terrain itself is grieving. The deity must fight through the forest's trauma without becoming part of it.

### Chapter 5 — The Crow-Crowned Geas Fracture
The Gate's final room is inside the deepest root — the heartwood of Avalora. The Crow-Crowned Geas Breaker waits there: a Hollow entity that has consumed enough Tuatha geas law to break any promise the deity makes. It does not attack directly; it breaks the deity's oaths, turning every promise of protection, restoration, and healing into a weapon used against the one who made it. The deity must win without making any promises — or win by making a promise so old and so deep that the Geas Breaker cannot find its roots to break it.

**Mission Focus:** Final confrontation. Boss: The Crow-Crowned Geas Breaker. Victory condition: the deity must win by relying on the terrain itself rather than their own power. The forest chooses to hold the Gate. The deity does not command it — they ask.

## Elite Campaign

Elite missions reinterpret the campaign as a sharper adaptive trial. The wound has learned from the Normal run:
- Safe roots become dangerous mid-mission
- The geas labyrinth changes which promises are still alive between missions
- Beast Realm predators adapt to the deity's Normal campaign tactics
- The Crow-Crowned Geas Breaker can now break promises the deity made in the Normal campaign

The finale proves that adaptation was not accidental. The deity must heal the wound twice — once against the Gate, once against the Gate's memory of being healed.
